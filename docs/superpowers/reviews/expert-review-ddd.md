# Expert DDD Review — `nexus-fulfillment` Example Application

Reviewer stance: adversarial, Evans/Verraes/Fowler/Noback-grounded. Scope: `examples/nexus-fulfillment/src/` at milestone 3, spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-03-nexus-fulfillment-example-design.md`. Adjudicated items (commands-as-contracts → M4, user-cancel-during-saga race, ContextBus at-most-once) are not re-litigated. All paths relative to `examples/nexus-fulfillment/`.

## Verdict

This is a better-than-average DDD teaching codebase with two structural faults that will actively mislead the companies who copy it. First, the `record()`-without-`apply()` convention breaks the foundational event-sourcing contract — "a command decides against the state produced by every event so far, including the ones this command just recorded" — and the `count($this->pending) === 1` dance in `FulfillmentProcess::confirmReservation` is the visible crack: aggregate authors must mentally pre-fold their own events, which is exactly the class of silent-wrongness a reference architecture exists to prevent. Second, the headline claim "Domain layer has zero framework imports (Deptrac-proven)" is not true as enforced: the Deptrac `Domain` rule whitelists `Nexus` wholesale, so the fence the tutorial will boast about is open. Around those two faults sit a cluster of important-but-fixable issues: rejection *reasons* are free-text prose (including milestone-roadmap references) persisted into immutable journals; rejection events are recorded for aggregates that never existed and are not idempotent under retry; the Orders/Inventory journals are literally composed of published contract classes, collapsing the internal-event/published-language distinction the spec promises to teach; and `FulfillmentCompleted`/terminal phases will not survive milestone 4's temporal modeling without renaming persisted history. The value-object discipline, actor-as-aggregate-boundary insight, rejection-as-event commitment, and honest race documentation are genuinely praiseworthy — which is why the remaining traps matter: readers will trust this code.

## Findings by Dimension

### 1. Aggregate design

**1.1 — Critical — `record()`-without-`apply()` breaks the aggregate's decide contract**
`src/Fulfillment/Domain/FulfillmentProcess.php:96-101`; convention declared in `src/SharedKernel/AggregateRoot.php:14-28` and `src/Orders/Domain/Order.php:28-35`.
Objection: an event-sourced aggregate's invariant is `state_for_next_decision = fold(history ++ newly_recorded)`. Here, methods read *pre-command* state after recording, so any method needing the effect of its first event before recording a second must simulate the fold by hand — the `count($this->pending) === 1` comment ("After apply(ReservationConfirmed), pending will be empty") is manual symbolic execution of `applyReservationConfirmed`. This works at N=2 events; at N=3 causally-chained events (M4 will have them: pick → maybe-complete → maybe-ship) authors will get it wrong silently, and there is no test that can generically catch "decided against stale state." The docblock's own concession — "semantically identical to the old OrderRules::decide()" — admits the deleted pure DECIDE/EVOLVE design (which the spec at lines 43-45 still describes!) was the honest shape.
Correct alternative (either): (a) Verraes/Broadway convention — `recordThat($event)` = `record` + `apply`, and change `AggregateBehavior`'s engine wiring so the evolve closure is applied only during replay/recovery, not to freshly persisted events (the double-apply hazard is a framework-integration artifact, not a law of nature); or (b) restore pure `decide(state, cmd): events` / `evolve(state, event): state` functions as the spec designed — the fold is then the engine's job in both paths and the trap is unrepresentable. Either way, delete the count===1 dance; it should be `if ($this->pending === [])` *after* applying, which reads as business language ("nothing left pending → complete").

**1.2 — Important — an invariant that throws instead of rejecting: mixed-currency order lines crash the actor**
`src/Orders/Domain/Order.php:200-212` (`computeTotal` → `Money::add`), `src/SharedKernel/Money.php:29-38`.
Objection: `place()` guards duplicate SKUs with a proper `OrderPlacementRejected` (praise), but an order whose lines mix EUR and USD reaches `Money::add`, which throws `InvalidArgumentException` — escaping the handler, crashing the actor into supervision/restart instead of recording a rejection. The example's whole thesis is "rejections are events, not exceptions" (spec line 53-55, Verraes); its own flagship aggregate violates it one invariant over. A company copying this learns "throw for the invariants I forgot to enumerate."
Correct alternative: validate currency homogeneity in `placeNew()` alongside the duplicate-SKU check and record `OrderPlacementRejected`. Teach the rule explicitly: *exceptions are for programmer errors and malformed VOs at the edge; every business 'no' inside a handler is an event.*

**1.3 — Important — `Order` at M3 is a status-flag machine, and `NotCreated` is a state of a thing that doesn't exist**
`src/Orders/Domain/OrderStatus.php:9`, `src/Orders/Domain/Order.php:62-64`.
Objection: with three transitions (`place`, `markStockReserved`, `cancel`) and one real invariant, `Order` is currently a state machine wearing an aggregate's clothes — acceptable at M3 *if named as such*, but the tutorial must not present it as the finished exhibit of a rich aggregate. Worse, `OrderStatus::NotCreated` models the aggregate's *absence* as a member of its own state enum. Evans: an aggregate's lifecycle begins at creation; "not created" is a fact about the repository/actor-shell, not about the order. It is forced by the spawn-on-demand-empty-instance actor pattern (defensible mechanically — Akka's `State.EMPTY` does the same), but the example never says so, so readers will copy `NotCreated` into enums where nothing forces it.
Correct alternative: keep the mechanism, name the trade-off in the tutorial ("the actor shell needs a pre-genesis value; we model it as `NotCreated` and treat it as *outside* the business lifecycle"), and consider `Order::notYetPlaced()` instead of `Order::empty()` so even the named constructor speaks the language.

**1.4 — Important — idempotent `place()` silently ignores a *conflicting* retry body**
`src/Orders/Domain/Order.php:70-72`; documented in `README.md:47-51` ("The retry body is ignored").
Objection: replying "accepted, here's the original order" when the retry carries *different lines* is not idempotency — it is swallowing a contradiction. True idempotency returns the same result for the same request; a different payload under the same `OrderId` is a distinct (erroneous) request and deserves a `409`/rejection. As written, a buggy client that reuses ULIDs gets silent data loss with a `201`.
Correct alternative: on `Placed`/`StockReserved`, compare the command's lines to `$this->lines`; equal → accepted-as-idempotent-repeat, different → reply rejected (no persist — see 5.2 for why not an event). This is one `if` and a far better lesson.

**1.5 — Minor — `AggregateRoot` puts persistence mechanics in the aggregate's public face**
`src/SharedKernel/AggregateRoot.php:20-27`.
Objection: `apply(object): void` is public, so any Application-layer code can mutate aggregate state around every invariant (`$order->apply(new OrderCancelled(...))` compiles and runs). `releaseEvents()` is engine plumbing. Noback would tolerate this as a pragmatic building block; Evans would ask why the domain's most public interface is about event plumbing rather than behavior.
Correct alternative: acceptable as-is *if* the tutorial names the exposure ("`apply()` is public for the engine; treat it as engine-only — nothing else may call it") — or move both methods behind a small trait + interface pair documented as "modeling building blocks," and see 4.1 for where that interface should live.

### 2. Ubiquitous language

**2.1 — Important — persisted reason strings contain developer-roadmap prose, frozen forever in the journal**
`src/Orders/Domain/Order.php:106` ("cancellation after stock reservation arrives in milestone 4"), `src/Orders/Infrastructure/Http/CancelOrderHandler.php:46` (`'api cancel'` as a persisted cancellation reason).
Objection: events are immutable business facts; these journals will contain the string "milestone 4" for the lifetime of the system, long after milestone 4 ships. "api cancel" is neither business language nor a code — a warehouse manager reading the timeline learns nothing. Verraes: events are the ubiquitous language's past tense; prose leaks the developer's context into the domain's memory.
Correct alternative: reason *codes* from the language — `CancellationReason::RequestedByCustomer`, `CancellationReason::OutOfStock` (the spec itself says `CancelOrder(reason: OutOfStock)` at line 88!), with optional free-text detail kept out of decision paths. See 3.1.

**2.2 — Important — `FulfillmentCompleted` / `FulfillmentPhase::Completed` are pre-emptively wrong names**
`src/Fulfillment/Domain/Event/FulfillmentCompleted.php`, `src/Fulfillment/Domain/FulfillmentPhase.php:16`.
Objection: at M3 "completed" means "all stock reserved," but the spec's fulfillment completes at *shipment* (spec lines 82-85). When M4-M6 add pick/pack/ship, either the event is renamed (breaking persisted history — `fulfillment.completed.v1` is already in journals) or "Completed" quietly changes meaning (worse). Event names must be timeless because journals are.
Correct alternative: name the fact that actually occurred — `StockFullyReserved` / `AllReservationsConfirmed`; phase `Reserving → StockReserved → …`. Rename now, while journals are dev-only; this is the cheapest moment it will ever be.

**2.3 — Important — `MarkStockReserved` is the orchestrator's technical voice, not the business's**
`src/Orders/Domain/Command/MarkStockReserved.php:15`.
Objection: "mark X" is CRUD-speak — setting a flag from outside. No domain expert says "mark the order stock-reserved." The saga is *informing* the order of a fact it observed. Additionally the contract docblock at `src/SharedKernel/Contracts/Orders/OrderStockReserved.php:11-16` says the *event* is "Emitted by the fulfillment saga" — false: it is recorded by the `Order` aggregate (`Order.php:88`) and published by the Orders context. Code and comments already disagree about who says what — the surest symptom of missing ubiquitous language.
Correct alternative: either name the command after the business occurrence the order must integrate (`ConfirmStockReservation`, or model it as the order *reacting to* the inventory facts), and fix the `OrderStockReserved` docblock to state the true emitter. Add the glossary doc the spec promises (`docs/ddd-influences.md`) with an event/command provenance table.

**2.4 — Minor — technical vocabulary in replies and constructors**
`src/Inventory/Application/Reply/StockCommandAccepted.php` / `StockCommandRejected.php` ("Command" is the messaging pattern, not the domain); `Order::empty()` / `InventoryItem::empty()` / `FulfillmentProcess::empty()` (technical genesis). Application-layer replies may be forgiven some plumbing tone, but a teaching example should show that even replies can speak the language (`ReservationOutcome`, `RestockConfirmed`).

**2.5 — Minor — spec/code vocabulary drift**
Spec line 42 promises `Placed → AwaitingStock → ReadyToPick → Picking → Packed → Shipped`; code has `placed / stock_reserved / cancelled`. Expected at M3, but the tutorial must reconcile — `AwaitingStock` vs `StockReserved` are different words for adjacent concepts, and readers holding spec and code side by side will wonder which is the language.

### 3. Value objects

**3.1 — Important — `string $reason` is primitive obsession at the heart of the pattern being showcased**
Every rejection event (`src/SharedKernel/Contracts/Orders/OrderPlacementRejected.php:23`, `OrderCancellationRejected.php:24`, `MarkStockReservedRejected.php:23`, `src/SharedKernel/Contracts/Inventory/StockReservationRejected.php:30`), `CancelOrder.php:15`, `OrderCancelled.php:21`, `RejectionEvent::reason(): string`.
Objection: the spec's proudest claim is "No primitive obsession, even on the wire" (line 175), yet the single most load-bearing field of the rejection-as-event pattern — the *why* — is a free string, interpolated ad hoc (`"duplicate SKU in order lines: {$line->sku->value}"`, `Order.php:186`), compared by nobody, and impossible to branch on. M4's compensation-vs-user-cancel logic will need to distinguish rejection causes; strings guarantee `str_contains` archaeology.
Correct alternative: a `Reason` VO (or per-context enums: `PlacementRejectionReason::DuplicateSku`, `ReservationRejectionReason::InsufficientStock`) with a machine code + optional human detail. `RejectionEvent::reason()` returns the VO; HTTP maps code → message. This also fixes 2.1 mechanically.

**3.2 — Important — inventory's core arithmetic runs on naked ints**
`src/Inventory/Domain/InventoryItem.php:47-52` (`int $onHand`, `array<string,int> $reservations`), `:62-73` (`reserved()`, `available()` return int), `src/SharedKernel/Contracts/Inventory/StockReservationRejected.php:29` (`int $available` with an apologetic comment: "Quantity cannot be 0").
Objection: the comment diagnoses the disease and prescribes nothing. `Quantity` (strictly positive) is the wrong VO for stock *levels* (zero is a legal and business-critical level: "out of stock"). Because no correct VO exists, the domain's most important numbers — on-hand, reserved, available — fall back to primitives, and `ReservationPolicy` compares `$requested->value <= $item->available()` across the VO boundary (`src/Inventory/Domain/ReservationPolicy.php:18`).
Correct alternative: a `StockLevel` VO (non-negative) with `plus(Quantity)`, `minus(Quantity)`, `isSufficientFor(Quantity): bool`; `reservations` as a first-class `Reservations` collection object (`holdFor(OrderId)`, `totalHeld()`). Then `ReservationPolicy::allows` reads `$item->available()->isSufficientFor($requested)` — the code becomes the sentence.

**3.3 — Minor — arithmetic gaps that M4 will hit**
`src/SharedKernel/Money.php` has no `subtract`, no `zero($currency)`, no allocation/split (partial shipments, refunds will need remainder-safe division of cents — the classic Fowler `allocate()` teaching moment). `src/SharedKernel/Quantity.php` has no `plus`/`minus`/`isGreaterThan` (partial picks: `picked + remaining = ordered`). Neither gap is wrong today; both are predictable M4 friction. Add with tests when needed, but reserve the API names now in the tutorial.

**3.4 — Minor — `OrderLine` in the SharedKernel is Orders vocabulary annexed by everyone**
`src/SharedKernel/OrderLine.php`. An order line (with `unitPrice`!) is Orders-context language. It sits in the kernel because `OrderPlaced` carries it (see 4.2). Inventory never needs a price; Fulfillment needs `(sku, quantity)` pairs. Kernel VOs should be the *truly* universal ones (`Money`, `Sku`, `Quantity`, ids); `OrderLine` is the first lodger that should have paid rent.

### 4. Bounded contexts & published language

**4.1 — Important — `AggregateRoot` (engine mechanics) lives in the SharedKernel**
`src/SharedKernel/AggregateRoot.php`, consumed by `src/Platform/Actor/AggregateBehavior.php`.
Objection: Evans's Shared Kernel is a *jointly-owned subset of the domain model* — language shared between contexts. `AggregateRoot` shares no language; it shares a persistence-engine calling convention (`releaseEvents()`, "MUST NOT be called from record()"). It sits in the kernel only so the deptrac fence lets Domain implement it — i.e., its location is dictated by plumbing, which is precisely the leak. The kernel is also already housing: VOs + all contracts of three contexts + `Bus/Publish` (a transport envelope, `src/SharedKernel/Bus/Publish.php`). That is a god-kernel trajectory.
Correct alternative: split explicitly — `SharedKernel/` (VOs only, jointly owned, hard to change — Evans's actual pattern), `Contracts/` promoted to a sibling top-level module documented as *Published Language* (a different Evans pattern; see 4.2), and `AggregateRoot` moved beside `AggregateBehavior` into `Platform/Actor` (or a `BuildingBlocks/` module) with a deptrac allowance `Domain → PlatformActor` *narrowed to that interface*. Naming the two patterns correctly is itself the teaching win — right now the example teaches "SharedKernel = the shared folder," which is the most common industry misreading of Evans.

**4.2 — Important — Orders and Inventory have no internal events: their journals ARE the published contracts**
`src/Orders/Domain/Order.php:12-17` (aggregate records `SharedKernel\Contracts\Orders\*` directly), `src/Inventory/Domain/InventoryItem.php:8-14`; contrast `src/Fulfillment/Domain/Event/*` (proper internal events, explicitly registered).
Objection: the persisted history of Orders is made of the exact classes other contexts consume. That welds internal remodeling to external contract versioning: rename a field for the aggregate's clarity → `orders.order_placed.v1` breaks for every consumer; enrich the contract for a consumer → the aggregate's journal schema changes. Fowler/Evans: a published language is *designed for consumers*, not an internal artifact re-exported. The example even demonstrates the right way in Fulfillment (internal events, no `#[MessageType]`, explicit registration) — so the codebase currently teaches both the pattern and the anti-pattern with no signpost saying which is which. Concretely: `OrderPlaced` carries full `OrderLine`s with `unitPrice` (`src/SharedKernel/Contracts/Orders/OrderPlaced.php:23-28`) — Inventory and Fulfillment now know what customers pay, purely because the internal fact was published wholesale.
Correct alternative: per-context internal events (in `Orders/Domain/Event/`), translated at the publish seam (the `withEventPublisher` closure in `OrderActor.php:53-55` is *already the perfect place* to map internal → contract, and to strip the price down to `(sku, quantity)` for the published `OrderPlaced`). If the shortcut is kept for example-size reasons, the tutorial must flag it in bold as a deliberate collapse, with the Fulfillment context shown as the grown-up version.

**4.3 — Minor — Fulfillment-as-context vs process-inside-Orders: right call, wrong silence**
`src/Fulfillment/` + `deptrac.yaml:99-105`.
Judgment: Fulfillment *is* a legitimate bounded context (it will own pick/ship policy) — the orchestrator location is defensible and better than hiding a process inside Orders. But at M3 the context contains exactly one process manager and zero domain concepts of its own beyond the phase enum, and its deptrac fence is the only one with cross-context Application imports (ref factories) — the adjudicated commands-as-contracts item covers the command classes, but the tutorial should also state why the saga holds *ref factories* (capability handles) rather than publishing commands to the bus, and what changes at the broker milestone. One paragraph; otherwise readers infer "sagas may import other contexts' Application layers" as a general license.

### 5. Rejection-as-event, fully applied

**5.1 — Critical — rejection events are persisted for aggregates that never existed**
`src/Orders/Domain/Order.php:102-104`: `cancel()` on `NotCreated` records `OrderCancellationRejected('Order does not exist')` — which `AggregateBehavior` persists (`AggregateBehavior.php:113-121`).
Objection: this creates a journal (and a snapshot cadence, and an actor) for an order that was never placed. (a) Semantically: Verraes's rejections-as-events records the *aggregate's* refusals — a thing that doesn't exist cannot refuse; the "fact" has no subject. (b) Operationally: `DELETE /api/orders/{random-ulid}` is unauthenticated-tenant-scoped but otherwise unbounded — any client with a valid token can mint unlimited persistent journals for phantom orders. That is storage amplification a reference architecture must not model.
Correct alternative: `NotCreated` + `cancel` → reply-only rejection, zero persistence: record nothing, and let `AggregateBehavior`'s empty-drain path reply `rejected` instead of `accepted` (needs a small seam — e.g. the aggregate exposing `exists(): bool`, or a `Decision` return, see 5.3). Persisted rejection events are for aggregates with history whose refusal is a business-relevant fact (cancel-after-reservation: yes; cancel-of-nothing: no).

**5.2 — Important — rejections are not idempotent, successes are: retried 'no's grow the journal unboundedly**
`src/Orders/Domain/Order.php:105-108` (every `cancel` on `StockReserved` records a fresh `OrderCancellationRejected`; ten retries → ten events); contrast `:72`, `:90`, `InventoryItem.php:82-84` (repeated successes are silent no-ops).
Objection: the journal-noise trade-off was accepted for *first* rejections, but nothing dedupes *repeat* rejections of the same command, so at-least-once delivery or client retries write the same non-fact repeatedly. Meanwhile the reviewer must also answer the posed question — is silence-on-repeat Verraes-honest, or should idempotent no-ops record `OrderAlreadyPlaced`? Verdict: **silence is correct.** A duplicate delivery is a transport phenomenon, not a business occurrence; recording `*AlreadyPlaced` facts would make journal size a function of network behavior and pollute replay with non-decisions. Verraes's "record the no" applies to *business* refusals, not dedup bookkeeping. But the same logic condemns repeated identical rejections: the first `OrderCancellationRejected` is a business fact; the ninth is transport noise.
Correct alternative: make rejection recording idempotent per (command-identity, state) — simplest: if the last journal event is an equal rejection for the same cause, reply rejected without persisting. Teach the symmetric rule: *first 'no' is a fact; repeated identical 'no's are noise on both the yes and no paths.* Observability (a metric/log for duplicate deliveries) replaces the journal for that concern.

**5.3 — Important — first-RejectionEvent-wins reply derivation is a fragile hidden contract**
`src/Platform/Actor/AggregateBehavior.php:104-121`.
Objection: whether a command "succeeded" is inferred by scanning drained events for the `RejectionEvent` marker; the first one found dictates the reply and its reason. This (a) forbids, silently, any future command that records a rejection *and* a success fact (partial acceptance — M4 partial shipments are the obvious candidate); (b) makes the reply reason whatever the first rejection interpolated, coupling HTTP responses to journal prose (see 3.1); (c) is invisible in the aggregate — the author of `place()` cannot see that recording order matters.
Correct alternative: make the decision explicit — intention methods (or the DECIDE function of 1.1's fix) return a small `Decision` value (`Decision::accepted(events)` / `Decision::rejected(reasonVo, events)`), and `AggregateBehavior` pattern-matches on it. Explicit beats marker-scan; the type also gives 5.1's reply-only rejections a natural home (`Decision::rejected($reason)` with no events).

**5.4 — Minor — asymmetric capture of 'no' across contexts**
Orders records three rejection kinds; Inventory records one (`StockReservationRejected`) but silently no-ops a `ReleaseReservation` for a nonexistent hold (`InventoryItem.php:104-106`) and can never reject a `Restock`. The release-of-nothing silence is defensible (compensation must be idempotent — releasing an unconfirmed hold is *designed* to be a no-op, `FulfillmentProcessActor.php:101-114`), but the asymmetry is undocumented; a reader cannot tell principled silence from forgotten rejection. Add the one-line rule per command in each aggregate's docblock: idempotent-by-design vs rejection-worthy.

### 6. Process manager

**6.1 — Important — `FulfillmentCompensated` is terminal-on-intent: the process declares victory before compensation happens**
`src/Fulfillment/Domain/FulfillmentProcess.php:109-121`, `:147-148`; side effects in `src/Fulfillment/Application/FulfillmentProcessActor.php:101-118`.
Objection: the event says "compensated" (past tense, fact) but records the *decision to compensate*; the `ReleaseReservation` tells are fired afterwards, at-most-once, unacknowledged. If one is lost, the journal's final word is `Compensated` while a hold leaks — the journal lies. Worse structurally: `Reserving → Completed | Compensated` with both terminal means M4's deadlines/retries have no state to live in — a phase machine with only terminal exits cannot model "compensating, awaiting N release confirmations, re-issue on timeout." This is the one place where the current shape actively blocks the spec's promised temporal modeling (spec lines 66-68, 88-90).
Correct alternative: split intent from outcome — `CompensationStarted` (phase `Compensating`, non-terminal, pending-release set in state) → consume `StockReleased` confirmations (the manager already routes inventory events; add `StockReleased`) → `CompensationCompleted` when the set drains. Deadlines then attach naturally to `Compensating` and `Reserving`. Same split applies to naming (see 2.2). Note also `FulfillmentPhase`'s enum cases are alphabetically sorted per code style — fine — but the docblock at `FulfillmentPhase.php:10` is the only statement of legal transitions; once phases multiply, encode transitions in a method (`canTransitionTo()`) so the state machine is testable, not prose.

**6.2 — Minor — call it a process manager and define both terms**
Spec/README say "saga" throughout; `FulfillmentProcess` is a *process manager* in the Fowler/messaging-patterns sense (central, stateful, event-in/command-out routing with its own persisted state) — a saga in the original Garcia-Molina/microservices sense is the compensation-chain concept. The example is actually a fine specimen of PM-with-compensation; the tutorial should spend the paragraph distinguishing them, because "saga" is the most abused word in this space and this codebase is positioned to teach it right.

**6.3 — Praise — foreign events as commands, own facts as journal**
`start(OrderPlaced)`, `confirmReservation(StockReserved)`, `rejectReservation(StockReservationRejected)` — the process consumes other contexts' *published facts* and records its own *observations* (`ReservationConfirmed`, not a re-persisted `StockReserved`). This is exactly right (a process manager's journal is its private memory of what it saw and decided) and is the strongest DDD move in the codebase. Also praiseworthy: the compensation release-set = confirmed ∪ pending (`FulfillmentProcessActor.php:101-114`) shows real reasoning about message races, and the residual sub-race is documented rather than hidden (`README.md:154-161`).

**6.4 — Minor — passivation timeout and business deadlines share one mechanism**
`AggregateBehavior.php:141-147, 220-226`: `ReceiveTimeout → Behavior::stopped()` for both entities and sagas. Fine today (respawn-on-demand + replay makes passivation transparent). But M4 deadlines will need durable timers that survive passivation (re-armed from state on recovery), and `AggregateBehavior` has no post-recovery hook to re-arm them. Not a bug; a missing seam. Design the recovery-hook signature *before* M4 rather than bolting it through the signal handler.

### 7. Layering (Noback)

**7.1 — Critical — the Deptrac `Domain` rule permits framework imports; the spec's headline guarantee is unenforced**
`deptrac.yaml:75-76`: comment says "Pure domain: PHP + SharedKernel value objects, nothing else"; rule says `Domain: [Domain, SharedKernel, Nexus, OrdersCtx, InventoryCtx, FulfillmentCtx]`. Spec success criterion (line 312): "Domain layer has zero framework imports (Deptrac-proven)."
Objection: `Nexus` in that list means a Domain class importing `ActorContext`, `Behavior`, or `EventStore` passes CI today. The domain files are currently clean (verified: `Order.php`, `InventoryItem.php`, `FulfillmentProcess.php` import only SharedKernel + SPL), so the allowance defends nothing and the comment above it is false. In a repo whose *product* is the enforcement pattern, a lying fence is worse than no fence — companies will copy the yaml verbatim.
Correct alternative: remove `Nexus` from the Domain ruleset (and from the context-fence lists' effect on Domain, via the cross-product notes already maintained). If some day a Domain file legitimately needs a Nexus type, that PR should hurt.

**7.2 — Important — `AggregateBehavior`'s signature-magic dispatch: acceptable machinery, unacceptable silence**
`src/Platform/Actor/AggregateBehavior.php:239-318` (discovery rules), `:256-276` (the caveat: "a public VOID method taking a non-message class WOULD be claimed").
Objection: convention-over-configuration dispatch is defensible engineering, but it is *implicit* in exactly the way DDD teaching warns against: the mapping from business command to intention method exists nowhere in the aggregate's text; it is inferred from `void` + single-concrete-class-param + public + non-static, with documented false-positive traps that only fail at runtime (or worse, silently claim a helper). Explicit-over-implicit (Noback) says the aggregate should declare its own protocol.
Correct alternative (pick one, teach it): (a) a `#[Handles]`/`#[When]` attribute on intention methods — one line, self-documenting, deletes all four heuristics and the ambiguity exception; or (b) an explicit `public static function handlers(): array` map. If the convention stays, the tutorial must present it as a *contract with sharp edges*, list the exclusion rules verbatim, and show the failure mode of an accidentally-claimed helper. What the tutorial should SAY, verbatim spirit: "The framework finds your intention methods by shape. That convenience costs you explicitness — here is the exact shape, here is how it breaks, and here is the attribute alternative if your team prefers to see the wiring."

**7.3 — Minor — HTTP handlers reach through Application into Domain commands**
`src/Orders/Infrastructure/Http/PlaceOrderHandler.php:11,47` constructs `Orders\Domain\Command\PlaceOrder` directly and `ask()`s the actor. Under this architecture the actor shell *is* the application service, so Infrastructure → Domain-command construction is the intended idiom — but Noback readers will flinch (Infrastructure instantiating Domain types as the *normal* path). One tutorial sentence legitimizes it: "commands are the application boundary's vocabulary; adapters build them, aggregates consume them." (Their planned M4 move to contracts also dissolves this.)

**7.4 — Praise — the composition root and the dual-fence Deptrac scheme**
`src/Platform/Boot/App.php` is a genuinely clean composition root (all wiring, zero logic; per-worker construction documented), and the structural-ring × context-fence dual-layer Deptrac scheme (`deptrac.yaml:57-105`) — including the honest comments about cross-product false positives — is a teachable artifact by itself, modulo 7.1.

### 8. Missing teaching concepts (for the tutorial roadmap)

1. **Repository, named** — `OrderRefFactory`/`InventoryRefFactory`/`ProcessRefFactory` *are* the Repository pattern in actor form (identity → handle to a consistency boundary, storage transparent). The example implements it well and never says the word. One docblock line each converts existing code into a taught concept.
2. **Factories for aggregate genesis** — everything begins at `::empty()` + a command; there is no example of an aggregate factory enforcing creation-time invariants (Evans ch. 6). `PlaceOrder` validation inside `placeNew` is halfway there; M4's `PickTask` creation (created *by* the process from an order) is the natural place to show a real factory.
3. **Specifications** — `ReservationPolicy` is a policy (good, named, but a static one-liner that cannot be parameterized — consider instance form with e.g. a safety-stock threshold to show Strategy-as-policy). A true Specification (`OrderIsCancellable`, composable, usable in both decide-paths and read-model queries) is absent and is the cheapest Evans pattern to add.
4. **Anti-corruption in miniature** — `FulfillmentProcess::applyFulfillmentStarted` translating foreign `OrderLine`s into its own `pending` map *is* a tiny ACL; naming it (a `ReservationPlan::fromOrderLines()` translator) would teach that consuming a contract ≠ adopting its model.
5. **Domain glossary + provenance table** — the promised `docs/ddd-influences.md` should include, per message: emitted-by, consumed-by, internal-vs-published, persisted-vs-transient. Findings 2.3/4.2 exist because that table doesn't yet.

## Top-5 Prioritized Recommendations

1. **Kill the pre-fold trap (1.1 + 5.3):** adopt apply-on-record (`recordThat`) with replay-only evolve, or restore pure DECIDE/EVOLVE returning an explicit `Decision` — delete the `count===1` dance and the first-rejection scan in one refactor. This is the single change that most affects what companies copy.
2. **Fix the Deptrac Domain rule (7.1):** remove `Nexus` from the `Domain` ruleset so the "zero framework imports, Deptrac-proven" claim is true. Ten-minute change, credibility-critical.
3. **Make reasons a VO and stop persisting phantom/duplicate rejections (3.1, 2.1, 5.1, 5.2):** reason codes + detail; reply-only rejection for `NotCreated`; idempotent rejection recording. The rejection-as-event showcase must be airtight — it is the example's signature pattern.
4. **Rename time-bombed events and de-terminalize compensation (2.2, 6.1):** `FulfillmentCompleted` → `StockFullyReserved`; `Compensated` split into `CompensationStarted`/`CompensationCompleted` with a `Compensating` phase — do it before M4 freezes journals and blocks deadline modeling.
5. **Separate internal events from published contracts in at least one entity context (4.2, 4.1):** give Orders internal events translated at the `withEventPublisher` seam, relocate `AggregateRoot` out of the SharedKernel, and rename/document `Contracts/` as Published Language. Fulfillment already models the right shape — make Orders match, or label the shortcut loudly.

## What Evans & Verraes Would Praise

- **Aggregates as physical consistency boundaries.** One actor per `(tenant, sku)` serializing hot-SKU contention (`InventoryItemActor`) is Evans's aggregate rule made *executable* — the invariant boundary and the concurrency boundary are the same object. Most DDD codebases simulate this with row locks; here the model and the mechanics coincide. This is the example's best argument for actors-for-DDD and deserves a whole tutorial section.
- **Rejections as first-class facts.** The `RejectionEvent` marker + persisted `StockReservationRejected` driving saga compensation *through the same event pipeline as successes* (`InventoryItemActor.php:29-32` — "the saga receives its failure signal without polling") is Verraes's pattern applied end-to-end, not as decoration.
- **Validating value objects on every edge** — `Sku`, `TenantId`, `OrderId`, `Money` self-validate in constructors; HTTP resolves path/body primitives into VOs before the domain sees them (`VoParamResolver`, `RestockHandler` 400-on-malformed-SKU); VOs travel on the wire via Valinor. Primitive obsession is genuinely absent at the boundaries (the gaps are interior — 3.1/3.2).
- **Per-context vocabulary for the same fact.** Inventory says `StockReserved`; the process records its own `ReservationConfirmed`. Two contexts, two languages, one occurrence — most examples get this wrong by sharing one event class.
- **Honest failure documentation.** The README's "Known limitations" (at-most-once seam, user-cancel race, compensation sub-race with the permanent-leak scenario) reads like an Evans-style context map of *temporal* boundaries — naming the races instead of hiding them is rarer and more valuable than fixing them silently, and it models the engineering culture the tutorial wants to transmit.
- **Small aggregates, no getter/setter bags.** `Order` at ~200 lines with `private(set)` state, behavior-only public surface (modulo `apply`), and intention methods that read as sentences (`place`, `cancel`, `confirmReservation`) — the object-design bones are right; the findings above are about the conventions wrapped around them.
